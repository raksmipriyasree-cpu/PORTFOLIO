TRIPS • VISITS • FIELD LEARNING

I learned in places too.

Some learning happened through real environments, conversations and exposure to people, technologies and pathways beyond the classroom.

01 / FARM VISIT

Designing for the reality of farming.

As part of the CRS project, I visited a farm and spoke with its owner about manual work, irrigation, crop challenges and where AI could — and could not — realistically help.

Open farm visit notes →
FIELD QUESTION

What can AI actually do?

The conversation highlighted a practical perspective: AI may help with predictions such as weather, irrigation timing and pesticide decisions, but it cannot simply replace every form of human judgement or labour.

PROJECT CONNECTION

From observation to an AI agent.

This visit gave the CRS project real user context for thinking about an AI agent designed around farmers’ needs.

02 / CHENNAI • 12–14 FEB 2026

Three days of universities, AI and new possibilities.

My Chennai trip combined university visits, technology exposure, industry interaction and a closer look at courses and career pathways.

Open my trip reflection →
DAY 01

MGR University of Research

We explored drones and drone-simulation technology, including how digital systems can support drone control.

DAY 02

Shiv Nadar University + industry

We explored admissions and courses, medical informatics using VR/AV systems, ECE and an industry session on practical AI applications.

DAY 03

VIT Vellore

We explored ECE equipment, 3D printing, AI in computer science, laboratories and the wider student environment.

WHAT STAYED WITH ME

Exposure changes what feels possible.

The trip broadened my understanding of AI, drones, medical informatics, electronics and engineering, while also making future academic choices feel more tangible.

TECHNOLOGY

AI beyond one subject.

I saw AI connected to computing, healthcare, industry and practical problem-solving.

PATHWAYS

Courses became real.

Seeing departments and talking through courses helped me connect present learning with possible future directions.

PRACTICAL LEARNING

Technology is easier to understand when you see it.

Drones, simulations, VR/AV systems and 3D printing gave me concrete reference points for ideas I had previously only encountered conceptually.

MEMORY

Learning travelled with the people around me.

The trip also strengthened friendships, teamwork and the sense that learning can happen through shared experiences.
