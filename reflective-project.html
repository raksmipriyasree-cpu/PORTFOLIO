IBCP CORE / REFLECTIVE PROJECT

AI, creativity & the future of design.

My research project examined whether companies are ethically justified in replacing human UI/UX designers with AI design tools.

CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION

“Should companies be ethically justified in replacing human UI/UX designers with AI design tools, given the implications for creativity, employment, and long-term sustainability?”

AIUI/UXETHICSEMPLOYMENTSUSTAINABILITY
RESEARCH JOURNEY

From technology to ethics.

At the beginning, I was mainly looking at the question as technological advancement. As I researched further, the issue became much more complex: efficiency had to be weighed against employment, creativity, ownership, accountability and the human role in design.

I used an interview with professional web designer Ms. Amrita from Cebra, a stakeholder survey comparing AI-generated and human-designed coffee shop websites, ethical frameworks and case studies to test the question from multiple perspectives.

KEY FINDINGS

The contradiction mattered.

01 / PRIMARY RESEARCH

Efficiency vs judgement

The interview suggested AI can reduce development time, while human input remains important for interpreting user needs.

02 / SURVEY

People liked AI output — but not replacement.

Participants often preferred the more appealing website output while still rejecting the idea that AI should replace human designers.

03 / ETHICS

Replacement is not neutral.

Utilitarian, rights-based and justice/fairness perspectives highlighted different costs, rights and distributions of benefits.

CONCLUSION

AI should augment, not replace.

My conclusion was that complete replacement of human UI/UX designers is not currently ethically justified. A more responsible and sustainable direction is collaboration: using AI to enhance creativity and efficiency while retaining human judgement, accountability and expertise.
